# Outfit Styling and Layout Rules

Load only when category-specific outfit or composition decisions are needed.

## Building Three Distinct Styles

- Keep the hero garment identical and faithful across all three images.
- Change the main bottom, footwear, and bag/accessory logic between styles.
- Give each style a different occasion and color strategy.
- Avoid three near-duplicates distinguished only by one accessory or accent color.
- Keep the season and target customer coherent unless the user asks for broader variation.
- Deliver each style as its own image, never as panels in one image.

## Outdoor and Shell Jackets

- Keep the jacket fully visible and functional.
- Build distinct technical, city-outdoor, and relaxed travel directions when suitable.
- Use streamlined technical pants, hiking trousers, leggings, or a simple outdoor skirt.
- Use practical sneakers, trail shoes, or light hiking shoes.
- Add at most one compact functional bag and one small lifestyle accent per look.

## Puffers and Winter Outerwear

- Give the bulky hero garment generous space.
- Distinguish sleek city, warm casual, and sporty outdoor directions when suitable.
- Pair with straight, tapered, or softly fitted bottoms.
- Avoid supporting pieces that compete visually with the puffer.

## Blazers and Commuter Jackets

- Consider polished office, relaxed smart casual, and youthful city looks.
- Use clean inner layers and high-waist trousers, skirts, dresses, or denim as appropriate.
- Vary loafers, low heels, flats, boots, or minimal sneakers across the set.

## Knitwear, Shirts, and Tops

- Balance a fitted top with a wider bottom; pair a loose top with a straighter bottom.
- Keep necklines, cuffs, hems, and construction lines unobstructed.
- Consider commuter tailoring, relaxed denim/cotton, and sporty or street bottoms when credible.
- Place the bottom nearby without overlapping the hero garment.

## Dresses and Skirts

- Keep accessories secondary to silhouette and fabric.
- Differentiate looks through footwear, outer layers, bags, and occasion rather than hiding the hero piece.
- Match footwear and bag scale to hem length and occasion.
- Do not cover the waist, neckline, or distinctive skirt structure.

## Sportswear

- Keep technical identity credible.
- Explore training, city athleisure, and relaxed travel directions when suitable.
- Use clean athletic shoes and minimal functional accessories.
- Avoid luxury accessories that conflict with sport positioning.

## Kidswear

- Prioritize age appropriateness, comfort, and activity.
- Differentiate school, weekend, and outdoor/play directions when suitable.
- Avoid adult styling, high heels, fragile jewelry, hard accessories, or overloaded bags.

## Color Strategy

Use a different controlled structure for each image when possible:

1. tonal family plus material contrast;
2. neutral base plus one small accent;
3. complementary color in low saturation;
4. hero color repeated once in shoes, socks, inner layer, or bag.

Do not add multiple bright accents unless the hero garment is playful and the target audience supports them.

## Wearable Accessories

- Include at least one and normally no more than two wearable accessories in every outfit image unless the user explicitly requests none.
- Choose accessories that support the style direction rather than filling space randomly. Useful categories include watches, earrings, necklaces, bracelets, belts, hats, scarves, socks, and eyewear.
- Prefer different primary accessory categories across the three looks when appropriate so the set feels intentionally styled.
- A watch should be shown as one complete product with the dial, case, crown, and full strap or bracelet visible. Keep it realistically smaller than shoes and bags.
- Treat paired jewelry as separate small products. Keep both pieces complete and separated by visible white background.
- Accessories must remain unworn, unobstructed, and independent. Do not place a belt through trousers, put eyewear on a hat, lay jewelry on clothing, or wrap a scarf around another product.
- Remove a second accessory before shrinking, touching, or overlapping products; the first purposeful accessory remains required.

## Composition Strategy

- Use a seamless, evenly lit, uniform pure-white (`#FFFFFF`) background in every image.
- Ignore the input image background completely. Never sample, inherit, match, imitate, or adapt its color, tone, texture, gradient, or lighting falloff.
- Do not use off-white, warm white, cream, ivory, light gray, beige, tinted, textured, gradient, vignette, or environmental backgrounds.
- Use a true straight-down 90-degree overhead view only; never use an angled camera or perspective scene.
- Prefer a compact, tidy, two-column asymmetrical composition rather than a rigid equal-cell grid or a loose scattered flat lay.
- Default occupancy pattern when product geometry permits: place the hero garment in the left main zone; place the inner top in the upper-right zone; place the bottom or longest supporting garment directly below it in the right main zone; align the two shoes separately along the lower-left; place the bag at lower-right; place the required one or two wearable accessories neatly in genuine residual gaps.
- Align nearby top edges, vertical centerlines, and lower anchors where natural product geometry allows. Use consistent visual gutters so the layout reads as deliberate and orderly.
- Preserve a clear size hierarchy: two large clothing shapes form the main balance, medium bag and shoes anchor the lower half, and any tiny accessory remains secondary.
- Let realistic dimensions control occupied area. A full-length dress, coat, skirt, or trouser may occupy more canvas area than a short hero jacket, while the hero remains the visual anchor through its upper-left placement and styling importance.
- Avoid identical box sizes or excessive symmetry, but also avoid drifting diagonals, random scattering, and large unused dead zones. The arrangement should feel compact and editorial while every product still owns an independent white-space cell.
- Adapt the pattern for unusually long or wide hero garments, but preserve its logic: two visually organized columns, closely spaced large shapes above or beside one another, and neatly aligned smaller products below.
- Preserve generous outer margins and consistent visible gaps between every item.
- Assign a separate non-intersecting layout cell to every individual product. Use compact consistent separation of roughly 1.5–2.5% of the shorter canvas edge where practical, including between the two shoes of a pair.
- Keep all products complete and fully inside the canvas; no product edge may touch the frame.
- Do not crop, overlap, stack, touch, hide, or obstruct any product.
- Sleeves, hems, trouser legs, straps, handles, accessories, and soft shadows must remain inside their own product cells and must not cross into another item's cell.
- Maintain believable relative product sizes and remove optional accessories if the composition becomes crowded.
- Keep photographic quality consistent while varying layout subtly across the three styles.
- Show products only. Never use a person, mannequin, body part, hanger, worn presentation, lifestyle scene, furniture, plant, book, cup, flower, food, cosmetic, electronic device, or decorative prop.
